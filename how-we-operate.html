How we operate

Three roles. One ecosystem.

Developers find and walk with entrepreneurs. Entrepreneurs build Kingdom businesses. Investors provide patient capital — and see the impact first-hand.

Developer Entrepreneur Investor Kingdom impact Get involved
01 · Developer

The bridge between entrepreneurs and investors.

An experienced businessperson with a strong instinct for identifying which businesses will succeed — committed to the long, demanding work of helping entrepreneurs grow until their businesses are sustainable.

Judging character well is essential. Business plans and training are the main forms of support, and because many businesses lack access to capital, the Developer links investors with entrepreneurs.

What a Developer does

  • Helps Biblically transformational businesses gain greater influence
  • Mentors and coaches local business people and entrepreneurs
  • Hosts international investors
  • Connects investors with local businesses — and follows up for accountability

Who we're looking for

  • A business background — formal study, or successfully owning or managing a business
  • A desire to serve God and depend on His wisdom and guidance
  • Strong written and verbal communication
  • Excellent cross-cultural skills
  • Good business judgement
  • The ability to turn a vision into a workable plan
  • A sense of adventure and willingness to travel

This is a volunteer role — not a paid position. Minimum 3 years commitment.

02 · Entrepreneur

Job makers, not job seekers.

We work with local believers who already own and run small or medium-sized businesses. We don't do microfinance — we back proven businesses ready to grow.

Heartfor God and for people
Headto think through business
Handsto run a business

Selection principles

  • The entrepreneur has already invested their own equity
  • The business has a proven track record
  • The entrepreneur is dedicated — contributing their own effort and assets

Over 200 entrepreneurs in eight countries have become B4T Businesses.

The selection process

A B4T Developer takes every potential entrepreneur through at least these steps:

  1. Know Your Client50 probing questions, with follow-up through pastors.
  2. Kingdom Impact Appraisal50 questions to confirm the business's Kingdom impact.
  3. Feasibility Plan QuestionnaireBusiness focus, SWOT, market and cash-flow analysis.
  4. Business SynopsisA one-page summary for potential investors.
  5. Full Kingdom Business PlanIncluding a 3-year cash-flow projection and P&L.

03 · Investor

Money that keeps working for the Kingdom.

Believers with financial resources who want their money to have ongoing, repeated Kingdom impact on investment.

“We business people have two pockets for our money.”

A small pocket for donations, and a larger one for investment. A donation is used once and is gone; an investment keeps working and can be reinvested again and again.

Ways to be involved

  • Investing — usually as loan capital, occasionally as equity
  • Personal involvement and coaching
  • Serving as a subject matter expert
  • Reinvesting money that is returned

How it works

  1. Join an investor tripTime with like-minded travellers, enjoy hospitality and introductions to local businesses.
  2. Meet entrepreneurs face to faceB4T screens out likely failures and prepares businesses for both profitability and Kingdom impact.
  3. Make your own decisionInvest peer-to-peer, directly in the business — as a pure investor or an equity partner.
  4. Developer follow-upThe Developer facilitates communication and accountability between both parties.

Investment amounts, terms and returns are discussed directly with the B4T team. Typical investments range from $5,000 to $100,000 per business.

Kingdom impact

A fourfold transformation.

Each entrepreneur is challenged to see their business as belonging to God, who calls and gifts them for business — glorifying Him through biblical principles in every area of life.

Spiritual

Running the business as worship, investing in employees and their families, and supporting local ministries from profits.

Economic

Sound practice, hard work and excellence that create wealth, employment and capacity to support the poor.

Social

Ethical dealings with clients, suppliers and employees; hiring disadvantaged people; taking part in community life.

Environmental

Managing resources soundly; products and methods that respect creation, reduce waste and improve efficiency.
