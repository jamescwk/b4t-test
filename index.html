What is B4T

Kingdom business, fulfilling its role in the Great Commission.

Vision

To see Kingdom Business fulfil its vital role in the Great Commission.

Mission

To empower indigenous believers and churches for the work of ministry, by multiplying Kingdom businesses in poor and unreached communities.

Ephesians 4:12

Our story

  1. 2008Early Business as Mission work within OM; Jakes Naudé joins the effort.
  2. 2010Co-founders Raymond Yap and Mike Lyth bring OM's Business as Mission efforts onto a common, more effective platform — B4T.
  3. Pioneering yearsTesting different ways of working to find what is most effective — "a road less travelled".
  4. TodayHundreds of Kingdom SMEs started across Africa, the Silk Road and Asia Pacific.

Our values

We listen to Jesus

B4T rests on the Lord's leadership. We consult Him often and rely on Him fully.

We trust one another

Trust built on competence and on following through on our commitments.

We do things well

"Our work is our worship" — reflecting God's glory and perfect work.

What makes us different

We are practitioners rather than teachers or trainers — much of what we do comes from field experience and lessons learned from our own mistakes.

1

Great Commandment + Great Commission

Loving God and neighbour (Mark 12:30–31) and making disciples (Matt 28:18–20) — through for-profit business with economic, social, environmental and spiritual impact.

2

B4T Developers

Skilled business people on the ground who identify, select and walk with indigenous entrepreneurs committed to Kingdom impact.

3

Local SMEs

Businesses owned by local believers who are part of the local church. SMEs create the most jobs — yet are the most vulnerable and under-served.

4

Kingdom stakeholders

Investors, trainers, Developers, board members and entrepreneurs — “the whole Church taking the whole gospel to the whole world” across the 10/40 window.

5

Ecosystem & P2P lending

A supportive community connecting entrepreneurs to resources. Investors lend peer-to-peer directly to each business — no central funding bottleneck.

6

Self-sustaining local entities

In each country, a local entity with its own independent board and a growing investment pool — autonomous regions bound by shared vision and values.

Four dimensions of impact

Spiritual

Business as worship — investing in employees and their families and supporting local ministries from profits.

Economic

Sound practice, hard work and excellence that create wealth, jobs and capacity to support the poor.

Social

Ethical dealings with clients, suppliers and staff; hiring the disadvantaged; taking part in community life.

Environmental

Sound resource management and products or methods that respect creation and reduce waste.
